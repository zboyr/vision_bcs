\title{AI-Driven Feline Body Condition Scoring:\\A Vision-Logic Fusion Approach}



\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Over 50\% of companion animals are considered overweight, yet identifying a pet's Body Condition Score (BCS) remains a challenge for non-professionals. We present an AI-driven pipeline for automated feline BCS assessment using the standardized 9-point scale. Our approach employs a Vision-Logic Fusion teacher-student distillation framework, where GPT-4o serves as a teacher to generate chain-of-thought reasoning data for fine-tuning the smaller Qwen3-VL-4B model. Preliminary results show that fine-tuned GPT-4o achieves a mean deviation of 0.36, outperforming veterinary students ($\sim$0.45) and approaching expert clinicians ($\sim$0.28). Initial fine-tuning of Qwen3-VL-4B reduced its mean deviation from 1.02 to 0.92, demonstrating the efficacy of task-specific adaptation with significant room for further optimization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The core problem addressed by this research is the critical lack of objective, accessible, and clinical-grade assessment tools for monitoring pet body condition in home and high-volume shelter environments. Currently, over 50\% of companion animals are considered overweight, leading to significant chronic metabolic health crises. Despite this prevalence, identifying a pet's Body Condition Score (BCS) remains a challenge for non-professionals who often rely on subjective observations easily deceived by breed-specific fur coverage.

This problem is worth solving for the following reasons. First, it democratizes veterinary diagnostics by providing a clinical-grade home-check tool for owners who lack training to identify skeletal markers beneath fur. Second, it acts as a first responder in under-resourced shelters, flagging high-risk or emaciated animals from intake photos to prioritize them for professional exams. Third, the research innovates by fusing visual feature extraction (e.g., hourglass shape) with structured metadata to compensate for the inability of 2D models to physically palpate ribs.

\section{Related Work}

Graff et al. \cite{graff2025} investigated inter-evaluator bias in feline BCS visual assessments, establishing that visual-only assessments have inherent variability among clinicians, which serves as the baseline for our AI comparison. Li et al. \cite{li2024clipfit} proposed ClipFit, a parameter-efficient method to fine-tun vision-language models by selectively updating bias terms and normalization layers, which informs our LoRA-based fine-tuning strategy for domain adaptation.

Jiang et al. \cite{jiang2025curriculum} introduced a two-stage curriculum distillation framework that first builds robust internal problem-solving in a student model before externalizing it as explicit reasoning, directly motivating our teacher-student pipeline design. Lee et al. \cite{lee2025customkd} proposed CustomKD, demonstrating that large vision foundation models can effectively boost edge model performance through knowledge distillation despite architectural discrepancies between teacher and student.

Zhang and Kanjo \cite{zhang2025tinyml} developed a multimodal TinyML system for livestock behavior recognition, demonstrating that compact models can achieve real-time animal health monitoring on resource-constrained hardware. Walczak et al. \cite{walczak2025bitmedvit} further showed that ternary-quantized Vision Transformers can perform structured medical image analysis on edge devices, supporting our goal of light deployment. Shao et al. \cite{shao2024visualcot} introduced the Visual CoT dataset, providing a framework for structured chain-of-thought reasoning in multimodal models that directly informs our GPT-4o prompting strategy. Deng et al. \cite{deng2024ape} surveyed multi-modal animal pose estimation methods, providing the foundations for identifying skeletal landmarks and body shape markers relevant to the assessment of BCS.

Jang et al. \cite{jang2025vl2lite} introduced VL2Lite, a knowledge distillation framework that transfers multimodal knowledge from large Vision-Language Models into lightweight networks via dual visual and linguistic alignment losses, achieving up to 7\% classification improvement without increasing inference cost. This directly informs our distillation strategy from GPT-4o to Qwen3-VL-4B. Yu et al. \cite{cotcd2025} proposed Chain-of-Thought Curriculum Distillation (CoTCD), combining CoT rationale transfer with curriculum learning to enable small student models to reach up to 94\% of a 540B teacher's performance on reasoning tasks. This validates our use of GPT-4o-generated reasoning chains as structured supervision for fine-tuning the student model. Kaya~G{\"u}la{\u{g}}{\i}z, F.  \cite{kaya2025promptml} systematically evaluated prompt engineering strategies — including few-shot prompting and Tree-of-Thought — for LLM-driven regression and classification tasks, finding that effective prompting combined with hyperparameter tuning yields competitive results comparable to AutoML baselines. This informs our prompt pipeline design for GPT-4o silver label generation. Shrivastava et al. \cite{shrivastava2025mllmprompt} benchmarked five multimodal prompt engineering techniques (ICL, CoT, SSR, ToT, and RAG) across diverse datasets and proposed the Greedy Prompt Engineering Strategy (Greedy PES) for adaptive prompt selection in MLLMs. This motivates our structured CoT prompting approach for eliciting clinically grounded BCS reasoning from GPT-4o.


\section{Dataset}

\subsection{Data Source and Composition}

The dataset is structured into two hierarchical tiers to balance rigorous validation with large-scale deep learning:

Validation & Methodology Set: This core set comprises 379 expert-verified canine images and 49 feline images (derived from the Graff et al. [1] BTS1-Quiz and DataS5 VA-BCS series). These images include diverse and challenging feline postures (e.g., dorsal, lateral, and seated views) and represent various breeds and coat types. The target variable is the Ground Truth (GT), originally calculated as the mean BCS score (1–9 scale) from two senior clinicians.

Large-Scale Training Set: To ensure generalization, the project incorporates approximately 1,000 additional pet images with gold-standard labels mapped to a three-category scale (Underweight, Ideal, Overweight). This set, partially sourced from open-source repositories (e.g., overweight-dogs), provides the volume necessary for fine-tuning the student model.

\subsection{Data Preprocessing and Transformation}

To bridge the gap between high-cost clinical labels and model performance, the preprocessing pipeline follows three critical phases:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Semantic Mapping (Regression to Classification):} To mitigate the high signal-to-noise ratio inherent in the 1–9 BCS scale—where boundaries between scores (e.g., BCS 4 vs. 5) are visually ambiguous without tactile sampling—all numerical scores were mapped into a \textbf{three-category classification}: \textit{Underweight}, \textit{Ideal}, and \textit{Overweight}. This transition stabilizes the model against interference from long fur or complex body contortions.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Reasoning Generation:} Rather than traditional silver-labeling, we employ \textbf{Gemini} (the teacher model) as a "logical translator." Gemini processes the images to generate \textbf{Synthetic Reasoning}—structured anatomical justifications (e.g., assessing scapular sharpness or neck fat distribution). This transforms the dataset from simple "image-label" pairs into "image-logic-label" triplets.
    \item \textbf{Robust Parsing and Feature Augmentation:} * \textbf{Robust Parsing:} Regular expressions are used to extract structured scores from the teacher model’s unstructured natural language outputs, ensuring data integrity across API responses.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Posture-aware Logic:} Features are augmented by enforcing specific observational priorities based on posture; for instance, in prone positions, the model is directed to de-emphasize obscured abdominal lines in favor of shoulder and neck contours.
        \item \textbf{Data Partitioning:} The processed data is split into an \textbf{8:2 training/testing ratio}, ensuring a uniform distribution of "hard samples" (e.g., long-haired or curled pets) across both subsets.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}


\section{Methodology}
To bridge the tactility gap---the inability of 2D models to physically palpate skeletal markers---we implement a Vision-Logic Fusion teacher-student distillation pipeline. This approach utilizes GPT-4o as a teacher to generate high-quality synthetic data for a student SLM (Qwen3-VL-4B) by aligning visual perception with clinical reasoning.

\subsection{Step 1: Synthetic Data Generation}
In the vision-logic synthesis stage, GPT-4o processes the core dataset of 50 images alongside structured metadata (breed, age, weight) to generate silver labels. The teacher model leverages this metadata to compensate for visual obstructions such as breed-specific fur, bridging the gap between visual cues and clinical standards. Using chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, the teacher identifies specific markers---such as whether ribs are visible from above or an abdominal tuck is present---before assigning a final score, providing the student model with a logic-based diagnostic path.

\subsection{Step 2: Data Formatting and Validation}
Synthetic examples are converted into a standardized JSONL format, pairing each original image with the teacher's visual-logic reasoning chain and final BCS label. A secondary critic pass, supplemented by human verification of a subset, filters out inconsistent generations to ensure the training data accurately reflects expert-level reasoning.

\subsection{Step 3: SLM Fine-Tuning}
Using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), we fine-tune the Qwen3-VL-4B model on the GPT-4o-generated Vision-Logic reasoning chains. This approach allows the SLM to internalize the diagnostic accuracy of the teacher model while remaining significantly faster and more cost-effective for local deployment. By focusing on this fusion strategy, a 4B parameter model can learn to perform specialized clinical assessments that would otherwise require a much larger model.

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experiment 1: Prompt Pipeline Tuning}

\subsubsection{Dataset Description}

Our evaluation uses a curated dataset of 50 cat photographs \cite{visionbcs2026}, with each image labeled on the standard BCS 1--9 scale. The samples are distributed across the score range to ensure a comprehensive assessment of model accuracy from underweight to obese categories.

\subsubsection{Methodology}

While prior work explored prompt tuning for improving accuracy, using an LLM as a single-step inference engine limits its capacity for self-correction. At this stage, we adopt a \textbf{Prompt Pipeline} architecture that decomposes the reasoning process into discrete, trackable steps, enabling iterative refinement of the model's inference.

Our methodology is to execute a set of prompt pipelines for each model against a subset of our dataset and compare their mean absolute deviation (MAD) against a single-step inference baseline. We then narrow down effective pipelines for full-dataset evaluation and iterate on prompt design and pipeline architecture to seek higher accuracy.

We constructed seven distinct sequential prompt pipelines. (1) Appearance Assessment V1 (AAV1) describes the cat's visual appearance, assesses it against BCS guidelines, then assigns a final score based solely on the resulting text. (2) Appearance Assessment V2 (AAV2) follows the same structure but additionally provides the original image as reference at the final scoring step. (3) Coarse Fine performs a coarse range selection, followed by fine-grained scoring and a final visual confirmation. (4) Majority of 5 runs five independent assessments and determines the final score by majority vote. (5) Per Score poses a series of Yes/No questions for each BCS level from 1 through 9 and selects the score accordingly. (6) Debate V1 begins with an initial assessment, argues for an alternative score, then adjudicates based on the reasoning. (7) Debate V2 follows the same structure as V1 with additional argumentation stages.

We selected five models spanning a broad range of open, closed, and edge deployments. The large models evaluated are GPT-4o, Gemini 3.1, Qwen3-VL-235B, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B. Qwen2.5-VL-3B serves as the small model representative.


\subsubsection{Results and Analysis}

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{prompt_pipeline_tuning_results_1.jpg}
    \caption{Pipeline evaluation results on the dataset subset.}
    \label{fig:prompt_pipeline1}
\end{figure}

All seven pipelines demonstrated general improvement over the single-step baseline on the dataset subset. Per Score and Coarse Fine were subsequently dropped, as both underperformed the baseline on at least one model. Debate V2 was also eliminated, as it offered no meaningful advantage over V1.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{prompt_pipeline_tuning_results_2.png}
    \caption{Pipeline evaluation results on the full dataset.}
    \label{fig:prompt_pipeline2}
\end{figure}

On the full dataset, Majority of 5 and Appearance Assessment V2 led consistently across models, while AAV1 fell below baseline --- suggesting it overfit to subset characteristics.

Pipeline effectiveness varied substantially by model: GPT-4o responded strongly to all pipelines, while Qwen models showed limited or negative responsiveness, with smaller models most susceptible to performance degradation.

Overall, prompt pipelines offer meaningful accuracy gains for frontier closed models but are unreliable or counterproductive for open-weight models, particularly smaller ones. Future work will explore pipeline designs better suited to open-weight architectures and investigate why structured prompting amplifies errors in smaller models.

\subsection{Experiment 2: LoRA Fine-Tuning with Distilled Labels}

\subsubsection{Dataset}

We constructed the \texttt{cat\_bcs\_10k} dataset \cite{catbcs10k2026}, comprising 10{,}000 cat photographs sourced from open repositories. Images were preprocessed using a two-stage pipeline: YOLO11 for object detection to crop individual cats, followed by ViTPose (pretrained on AP-10K) for pose estimation to filter images where the body and at least three legs are visible---ensuring sufficient anatomical information for BCS assessment. BCS annotations were generated by Gemini~3.1~Pro via the OpenRouter API. Each annotation includes the predicted primary and secondary BCS scores (1--9), free-text reasoning, confidence rating (1--9), confidence detractors, and breed identification. All 10{,}000 images yielded valid annotations. The dataset was split 95/5 for training and evaluation.

\subsubsection{Methodology}

We fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct using 4-bit NF4 quantization with LoRA (rank~16, $\alpha$=32) applied to all attention and MLP projection layers of the language model. The vision encoder remains frozen. Training uses SFTTrainer with completion-only loss masking---labels are set to $-100$ for all system, user, and image tokens so that the cross-entropy loss flows exclusively through the assistant's JSON response. Key hyperparameters: learning rate $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay, effective batch size 8 (gradient accumulation), bf16 mixed precision.

To study data scaling and overfitting, we run two configurations: (1)~500 samples for up to 6 epochs, and (2)~the full 9{,}500 training samples for up to 3~epochs.

\subsubsection{Results}

\paragraph{In-Domain Evaluation.}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{lora_indomain_eval.png}
    \caption{In-domain MAE on the cat\_10k held-out eval set (500 samples). Red bars: 500-sample training subset; blue bars: full 9{,}500-sample training set. Data scale dominates training duration.}
    \label{fig:lora_indomain}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:lora_indomain} summarizes the in-domain results. The base Qwen2.5-VL-3B model achieves an MAE of 1.56. With 500 training samples, MAE drops rapidly to 0.70 after 2 epochs but plateaus at $\sim$0.62 despite training for 6 epochs---a clear overfitting signal. Scaling to 9{,}500 training samples breaks through this plateau: MAE reaches 0.480 after just 1 epoch, improving to 0.454 at epoch~2 and 0.428 at epoch~3, a 73\% overall reduction from the base model.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{lora_scaling_and_essay.png}
    \caption{(a) MAE trajectories for 500-sample vs.\ 9{,}500-sample training, showing data-limited overfitting vs.\ continued improvement. (b) Cross-domain evaluation on the Essay benchmark comparing our fine-tuned model against frontier LMMs and base SLMs.}
    \label{fig:lora_scaling_essay}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:lora_scaling_essay}(a) contrasts the two training regimes. The 500-sample curve flattens after epoch~2 while training loss continues to decrease---the hallmark of overfitting to a small dataset. The 9{,}500-sample curve shows sustained improvement with no sign of plateau at epoch~3.

\subsubsection{Discussion}

Three findings emerge from this experiment:

\textbf{Data scale dominates training duration.} The 500-sample experiment hits a ceiling (MAE~0.62) that no amount of additional training can break, while a single epoch on 9{,}500 samples surpasses it (MAE~0.48). This confirms that the teacher's knowledge transfer is limited by the diversity of the training distribution, not by model capacity.

\textbf{Teacher style leaks into the student.} The fine-tuned model inherits the teacher's scoring biases. On the Essay benchmark, its error pattern (center regression toward BCS~5) mirrors Gemini~3.1~Pro's known tendency to under-predict extreme scores. This is consistent with the distillation paradigm: the student learns the teacher's policy, not the ground truth.

\textbf{Domain shift remains the primary bottleneck.} The gap between in-domain MAE (0.428) and cross-domain Essay MAE (0.980) is $2.3\times$. On the synthetic Purina~3D benchmark (3D-rendered cats), the model's MAE rises to 1.88, with 90\% of predictions collapsing to BCS~5. This indicates that LoRA adaptation to natural photographs does not generalize to synthetic imagery, and underscores the need for multi-domain training data.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

Building on the initial benchmarking results and the developed pipeline, our objectives include the following.

\textit{Vision-Logic Fusion Training.} We aim to complete the full fine-tuning cycle for Qwen3-VL-4B using the GPT-4o-generated CoT dataset, evaluating robustness for edge cases such as all-black or long-haired cats.

\textit{Ablation Study.} We will compare SLM performance with image-only versus image-plus-metadata inputs to quantify the impact of the LLM logic layer on reducing MAE.

\textit{Performance Benchmarking.} We will validate whether the fine-tuned SLM can bridge the performance gap, aiming to bring its MAE ($\sim$0.86) closer to GPT-4o ($\sim$0.43) and human experts ($\sim$0.33).

\textit{Consistency Validation.} Repeat image pairs (e.g., cats 2/50 and 5/47) will be used to ensure stable, reproducible scores across sessions.

\textit{Real-World Deployment.} Finally, we plan to integrate the fine-tuned SLM into a pet health application for beta testing by pet owners and shelter staff.
